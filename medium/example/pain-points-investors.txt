**Key Pain Points of Investors in A-Shares (from the Video & Broader Context)**

The video (by Justin Sun's channel) heavily criticizes the structural issues in China's A-share market. After 10 years of studying financial reports, the speaker concludes that most retail investors are essentially "there to settle the bill" (來結賬) — meaning they provide liquidity and capital for others (companies, insiders, government financing needs) but often end up losing.

### 1. **A-Shares as a Financing Market, Not an Investment Market**
   - **Core Pain**: The market's primary function is to help companies (especially SOEs and new listings) raise capital, rather than reward long-term shareholders with sustainable returns.
   - Frequent IPOs, refinancings, and dilutions transfer wealth from retail investors to listed entities.
   - Low dividend payouts and poor shareholder protections compared to mature markets like the US.
   - Result: Even when the index rises, many individual stocks underperform or lead to losses for buy-and-hold investors.

### 2. **High Volatility and Policy-Driven Uncertainty**
   - **Pain**: Heavy government intervention (stimulus, crackdowns, "national team" buying/selling) creates unpredictable swings. Investors feel they are gambling on policy rather than fundamentals.
   - Retail-heavy market (high participation from unsophisticated individuals) amplifies speculation, bubbles, and crashes.
   - Rich/sophisticated investors see this and prefer holding cash, overseas assets, or crypto for better risk-reward and control.

### 3. **Poor Returns and "Settlement" Dynamic for Retail**
   - After years of research, the realization that you're often the exit liquidity for smarter players or state needs.
   - Historical performance: Many retail investors lose money over cycles despite occasional bull runs.
   - **Wealth transfer**: From small retail to institutions, insiders, or during financing events.

### 4. **Loss of Overseas Investment Channels (Recent 2026 Crackdown)**
   - The 1 trillion USD repatriation stems from shutting down platforms like Futu, Tiger Brokers, and Longbridge.
   - **Pain for Investors**: Forced to sell overseas holdings (US/HK stocks) over 2 years — "sell only, no buy." They miss out on strong performers like Nasdaq tech stocks while being pushed back into A-shares they distrust.
   - Limited legal alternatives (QDII, Stock Connect) have quotas, higher costs, and fewer choices.

### 5. **Broader Economic & Trust Issues**
   - **Capital Controls & Safety**: Fear of further restrictions, currency volatility from mass repatriation (RMB pressure, inflation/consumption impacts).
   - **Lack of Confidence**: Property slump + weak economy makes domestic assets unappealing. Rich people hold cash/crypto to preserve optionality for future outflows.
   - Low financial literacy among many retail investors exacerbates behavioral biases and losses.

### Why Rich People Prefer Holding Cash/Crypto
They've "seen the world" (overseas markets with better governance, dividends, and growth stories). They view A-shares as too risky/speculative and prefer liquidity + diversification over forced domestic exposure.

**Overall Sentiment in the Video**: The repatriation won't sustainably "save" A-shares without deep reforms. It may create short-term liquidity but reinforces the "settling the bill" trap for ordinary investors.
